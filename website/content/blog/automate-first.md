Automation is most valuable where work is frequent, rule-based and error-prone. Here is a simple way to find those
tasks and decide where to start.

## List the repeatable tasks

Ask each team for the tasks they do the same way every week: data entry, reports, reminders, approvals, moving
information between systems. Write down how often each happens and how long it takes.

## Score them

For each task, consider three things:

- **Frequency:** how often does it happen?
- **Rules:** can the steps be written down without "it depends"?
- **Risk:** what does a mistake cost?

Tasks that are frequent, rule-based and costly when wrong are the best first candidates.

## Good first projects

- Collecting and filing documents that arrive by email
- Weekly or monthly reports built from the same sources
- Notifications and reminders based on dates or status changes
- Keeping customer or product data in sync between two systems

## What to leave for later

Processes that change often, rely on judgement, or happen only a few times a year rarely repay the effort. Improve
the process first; automate it once it is stable.

## Keep a person in the loop

Good automation handles the routine and hands exceptions to a person, with the context needed to decide quickly.
